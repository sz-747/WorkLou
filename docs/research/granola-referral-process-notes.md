# Granola Notes: Referral Process Improvement

**Source:** Interview summary supplied by Steve

**Captured:** 5 September 2026
**Evidence type:** Meeting-reported. These notes describe staff experience and have not been independently measured.

## Referral research pain points

Finding the right service is the biggest time drain.

- Caseworkers rely on an outdated Excel spreadsheet that is rarely maintained because of workload.
- Services change capacity and eligibility criteria constantly.
- A woman may be referred to seven or more services before one confirms availability.
- Eligibility criteria vary widely across refuges.
- Some accept only single women. Some exclude alcohol or other drug use, children, or animals.
- Women on nil income need specific beds. Centrelink recipients may pay 20% of income for refuge accommodation.
- Women on complex visas are ineligible for many mainstream services and may require "back door" routes.
- Service information is often wrong or outdated.
- At the end of the financial year, many services run out of capacity without advertising it.
- Time pressure means caseworkers scan websites quickly and may refer to unsuitable services, causing women to bounce between places.

## Follow-up burden

- Referrals are tracked by email rather than phone so there is a paper trail.
- A referral may require follow-up every five days for up to five weeks.
- Crisis accommodation is the exception. It requires direct calling, often daily, to secure a bed.

## Case management and documentation

- Caseworkers take bullet-point notes during appointments, then write full case notes and support letters afterward.
- There are no fixed plans. Every plan is tailored to the woman's situation, such as an exit plan, safety plan, bank accounts, phone safety, and support network.
- Case notes are stored on file. Historical data exists but has not been used beyond individual records.
- The programs manager spends significant time helping caseworkers with complex cases.
- The programs manager acts as a senior sounding board, reviews all issues a woman is experiencing, and checks that every action is covered.
- One proposed idea is a checklist where caseworkers select presenting issues, such as domestic violence or homelessness, and the system surfaces relevant actions and considerations.

## Warm referrals and accurate summaries

- Summary profiles are valuable for warm referrals.
- Re-telling trauma repeatedly is harmful. A transferable summary can reduce the need for a woman to repeat her story.
- Workers can add their own interpretation, so the summary must preserve what the woman actually said.

## AI opportunities identified

### Resource database

- Generate a service profile from a typed service name, including eligibility, referral process, and wait times.
- New services are currently discovered through Google and word of mouth.
- Enriching a service name into a useful profile could save significant research time.

### Bullet points to case notes

- Turn about ten bullet points into a trauma-informed case note or support letter.
- Staff currently do this manually in ChatGPT without identifying information.
- The request is for a secure, purpose-built tool.

### Preferred language

- Terminology changes over time, including language around alcohol and other drug use.
- Staff need a way to teach the tool their preferred language.

### Privacy and future learning

- The team currently avoids putting identifying information into ChatGPT.
- The interview noted that provider training can be disabled, but this alone does not settle privacy, storage, retention, or access decisions.
- Longer term, authorised and properly de-identified historical case notes might help improve best-practice guidance. This requires a separate privacy and governance process.
